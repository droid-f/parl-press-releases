Foreign Affairs Committee focuses on multilateralism during fact-finding trip to the US

A delegation of the National Council's Foreign Affairs Committee (FAC-N), headed by its president National Council member Laurent Wehrli, visited the United States from 5 to 10 May. The trip provided an opportunity to discuss various aspects of multilateral diplomacy, bilateral relations between Switzerland and the US, and American politics and economics. The delegation travelled to Washington, D.C. for talks on US policy in this election year, and also held discussions with representatives of the World Bank and the International Monetary Fund. It then continued to New York, where the focus was on Switzerland's mandate on the United Nations Security Council, before heading to Dallas (Texas) to learn more about business and research in the US.

Content

Multilateral institutions and the challenges they face were high on the delegation's agenda. With Switzerland currently a non-permanent member of the UN Security Council, the delegation visited the Permanent Mission of Switzerland to the UN. In the course of several meetings, it was able to gain a clearer idea of how the Security Council works and of the work of the Mission and Swiss diplomats in New York. The delegation also met representatives of former non-permanent members of the Security Council to learn about their experiences. The possibilities for action by small and neutral countries, the reform efforts initiated or supported by the non-permanent members and the extent to which the various national parliaments are involved were also discussed. Finally, the delegation had the opportunity to talk briefly with the president of the General Assembly, His Excellency Dennis Francis, who is the permanent representative of Trinidad and Tobago to the United Nations.

During the stopover in Washington, D.C. the delegation held talks with Swiss representatives of the International Monetary Fund (IMF). Marcel Peter, Switzerland's representative on the IMF's Executive Board, gave an overview of the Fund's tasks and operations and outlined a number of current issues. Among other things, he expressed his concern about the growing debt of many of the world's poorest countries, and explained how the IMF’s Resilience and Sustainability Trust (RST) works. The RST, to which Switzerland contributes, provides loans to support macroeconomic reforms and longer-term structural measures.

In a meeting with World Bank Managing Director Ajay Banga, the latter explained his priorities for the institution, which include improving efficiency, coordinating more closely with other international organisations involved in development aid, and taking greater account of challenges such as climate change in the bank's activities.

The Swiss delegation continued its discussions on this subject in New York, in particular during a meeting with Achim Steiner, Director of the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP). Mr Steiner explained the delicate situation in which his organisation finds itself, its funding threatened by the fall in development aid budgets in contributing countries. He also thanked Switzerland for its commitment to the organisation.

The delegation held various meetings on political and economic relations between Switzerland and the United States during its trip. In Dallas and Washington, D.C. the delegation met representatives of Swiss companies based in the US. With the aim of learning more about how research operates in the US, the delegation also visited the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center, where it met Swiss researchers working for the institution.

Finally, back in Washington, D.C. the delegation members took a closer look at American political processes. Staff from the House of Representatives Committee on Foreign Affairs and the Library of Congress briefed the delegation on the particularities and subtleties of political life in the United States.

Each year each of the foreign affairs committees sends a delegation abroad on a fact-finding mission. This year the FAC-N delegation is led by its president, National Council member Laurent Wehrli (FDP/VD), accompanied by National Council members Claudia Friedl (SP/SG), Corina Gredig (GLP/ZH), Monika Rüegger (SVP/OW), Roland Büchel (SVP/SG), Niklaus-Samuel Gugger (EVP/ZH), Fabian Molina (SP/ZH), Erich Vontobel (EDU/ZH) and Nicolas Walder (Greens/GE).
