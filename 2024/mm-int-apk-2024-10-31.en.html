Strategic horizons: Focus of third Parliamentary Foreign Policy Day on Europe and the Asia-Pacific region

This year's Parliamentary Foreign Policy Day on 6 November will be dedicated to significant and urgent challenges in both the European context and the Asia-Pacific region. The event, which is being organised in collaboration with the Geneva Centre for Security Policy (GCSP), will be chaired by the presidents of each of the foreign affairs committees, National Council member Laurent Wehrli (FDP, VD) and Council of States member Marco Chiesa (SVP, TI). In addition to high-profile speakers from the diplomatic and academic communities, Petr Pavel, President of the Czech Republic, will be guest speaker.

Content

The third Parliamentary Foreign Policy Day will open on 6 November with an address by the president of the Czech Republic, Petr Pavel, who is on a state visit to Switzerland. The day will be divided into two thematic blocks. The first of these will be dedicated to Russia's war against Ukraine. This marks a watershed for peace and security in Europe, having influenced foreign and security policy thinking and action in European capitals since its outbreak in February 2022. Since then, the situation on the ground has continued to evolve, as have political developments in countries such as the USA, where the elections on 5 November could have a decisive influence on the course of the conflict. Participants will hear papers by Ambassador Thomas Greminger, Director of the GCSP, Thomas Graham, a former senior US official and expert on Russia, and Daniel Möckli, head of the Center for Security Studies (CSS), a think tank at ETH Zurich, addressing among others the following questions: What does the future of war and its various levels of conflict look like? What form might a ceasefire take and what steps are needed for a conflict resolution process? What role can and should neutral Switzerland play? What conclusions can be drawn for Switzerland's parliamentary foreign policy?

The second block will focus on Asia's geopolitical influence. The region has become the primary arena for the intensifying superpower rivalry between the USA and China. Asia is not only home to the world's fastest growing markets, but also to security hotspots of global significance. These include the Strait of Taiwan, the Korean Peninsula and the South China Sea. This thematic block will begin with a lecture by Kyung-wha Kang (President and CEO of the Asian Society, Foreign Minister of South Korea 2017–2021) and a panel discussion with Antoine Bondaz (Director of the Indo Pacific Observatory at the French think tank Fondation pour la Recherche Stratégique), Bernardino Regazzoni (Swiss ambassador to China until 2022) and Zichen Wang (expert at the Chinese think tank Center for China and Globalization). The participants will discuss the close link between prosperity and security in Europe and security trends in Asia. What opportunities for influence and bilateral and multilateral cooperation do European states have in the rapidly changing regional order, from which they have been largely excluded in recent decades? What conclusions can be drawn for Switzerland's parliamentary foreign policy?

INVITATION TO THE MEDIA
