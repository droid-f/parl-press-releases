Press release

The presidents of the parliaments of Mongolia and Hungary received in Bern

The president of the National Council, Marina Carobbio Guscetti (SP/TI), will receive the presidents of the parliaments of Mongolia and Hungary and their delegations during the coming spring session. The Mongolian visit will take place from 12 to 14 March and the Hungarian visitors will be in Bern from 18 to 21 March.

Content

​Mongolia

On 13 March Marina Carobbio Guscetti, the president of the National Council, will welcome to Bern Gombojav Zandanshatar, who was elected president of the Mongolian parliament, the State Great Khural, on 1 February of this year. This is Mr Zandanshatar’s first official voyage abroad as president, and will provide an opportunity to renew and relaunch relations between the parliaments of Switzerland and Mongolia. The Swiss Parliamentary Services and the secretariat of the Mongolian parliament are currently jointly engaged in a technical cooperation project on public policy assessment, part of the Swiss Agency for Development and Cooperation’s support for the Mongolian parliament.

The last meeting between the two parliaments was in 2008, when the then president of the National Council, André Bugnon (SVP/VD), made an official visit to the Mongolian parliament with the aim of strengthening parliamentary relations between the two countries.

Relations between Switzerland and Mongolia date back to the late 1950s, when the first Swiss businesses set up commercial relations with the Asian country. Switzerland was Mongolia’s first non-socialist commercial partner. It officially recognised the Democratic Republic of Mongolia on 20 May 1964. Relations have become closer since Mongolia embraced democracy in the early 1990s. When Mongolia suffered some particularly harsh winters at the end of that decade, Switzerland provided humanitarian aid, and since 2002 this support has taken the form of development cooperation.

Reciprocalvisit

On 20 March Ms Carobbio will meet the president of the Hungarian National Assembly, László Kövér. Mr Kövér received the then president of the National Council, Christa Markwalder (FDP/BE), in Budapest in 2016, to mark the 60th anniversary of the Hungarian Revolution. During the 1956 uprising, some 12,000 of the Hungarians who fled from the Soviet troops found refuge in Switzerland.

Ms Carobbio and Mr Kövér will focus their discussions on bilateral and interparliamentary relations between the two countries. They will also discuss current European affairs, in particular Brexit, the European elections in 2019, Swiss-EU relations, and the issue of migration.

Hungary and Switzerland have enjoyed good relations for many years, and there is considerable exchange between their governments, administrations and parliaments. Between 2007 and 2012, Switzerland contributed CHF 131 million to 39 projects in Hungary under the EU enlargement programme.

Economic relations between the two countries are also important. In 2017, the total volume of trade rose to CHF 2.11 billion, placing Hungary third among Switzerland’s commercial partners in Central Europe, after Poland and the Czech Republic.
