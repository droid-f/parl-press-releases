Working visit to Berlin by FAC-S focuses on ‘watershed era’

A six-member delegation representing the Council of States Foreign Affairs Committee (FAC-S) paid a working visit to Berlin from 21 to 23 November. Led by FAC-S President and Council of States member Pirmin Bischof, the delegation met with members of the German federal government, the Bundestag and the Bundesrat (Chamber of States), as well as representatives from several ministries. Swiss¬–German relations stood at the heart of the meetings, along with the war in Ukraine, the energy crisis and challenges in European policy and geostrategy.

On 27 February, just a few days after the Russian invasion of Ukraine, German Chancellor Olaf Scholz delivered his policy statement. In this speech, he announced a profound change in Germany’s approach to security and foreign policy, stating that Europe has now entered a ‘watershed era’. The FAC-S delegation’s visit to Berlin also concentrated largely on the consequences that the war of aggression in Ukraine has had on German and Swiss policy.

The delegation met with representatives of their parliamentary counterparts, the Bundesrat Committee on Foreign Affairs, led by Michael Kretschmer, prime minister of Saxony. They also held talks with representatives of the German Bundestag, including members of the Foreign Affairs Committee, the European Affairs Committee and the parliamentary group on Swiss–German relations.

In addition to their meetings at parliamentary level, the delegation met with Federal Minister of Finance Christian Lindner, with discussions focused primarily on current topics in fiscal policy. The German government has undertaken various additional spending measures in response to this ‘watershed era’, including an additional EUR 100 billion for the Bundeswehr and EUR 200 billion in energy subsidies for consumers and businesses.

The delegation met with representatives of the German Chancellery (State Secretary Jörg Kukies), the Federal Ministry for Economic Affairs and Climate Action (State Secretary Stefan Wenzel) and the Federal Foreign Office (Ambassador Birgitt Ory). The delegation later visited a Stadler Rail Deutschland plant and met with experts from business, research and culture.

These meetings consistently showed that Switzerland and Germany enjoy very close and friendly relations, and that both countries are currently facing similar difficulties in many areas – in particular with respect to the impact of the war in Ukraine and its consequences for their respective energy and climate policies. Consideration was given to how Europe could increase its strategic autonomy in light of the shifting geopolitical situation, not only concerning Russia but also China and the US. Various discussions touching on Swiss neutrality, Swiss policy on sanctions and the ban on transferring Swiss war materiel to Ukraine provided an opportunity to clarify some misunderstandings. Swiss–EU relations were a central topic, as frequent mention was made of the importance of stable relations for continued success in business and research cooperation, as well the need for solutions to curb irregular migration at European level.

The Foreign Affairs Committees generally send delegations abroad annually. This year’s FAC-S delegation was headed by Committee President Pirmin Bischof (The Centre/SO) and comprised of Council of States members Andrea Caroni (FDP/AR), Andrea Gmür-Schönenberger (The Centre/LU), Daniel Jositsch (SP/ZH), Matthias Michel (FDP/ZG) and Thomas Minder (independent/SH).
