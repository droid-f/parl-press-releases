Visits from abroad

Council presidents to visit international organisations in Geneva

National Council president Irène Kälin (The Greens/AG), and Council of States president Thomas Hefti (FDP/GL), were in Geneva on 3 and 4 November to meet Volker Türk, the new United National High Commissioner for Human Rights, and Gilles Carbonnier, vice president of the International Committee of the Red Cross (ICRC). On 4 November they also met the president of the Geneva cantonal government, Mauro Poggia and Cantonal Councillor Nathalie Fontanet.

Content

Upon their arrival in Geneva, the Council presidents met with representatives of the Permanent Mission of Switzerland to the UN and other international organisations represented in the city to discuss the challenges and possibilities of multilateralism. Ambassador Jörg Lauber outlined the key features of International Geneva, which make it one of the main centres of global governance and host to 42 international organisations, 178 states and 750 NGOs. He also highlighted Switzerland’s strategic role as host nation. Humanitarian cooperation and the questioning by some donors of the principle of the ICRC's impartiality towards the parties to the conflict in Ukraine were also discussed, as was the increasing politicisation of the World Health Organization (WHO). The Council presidents were also briefed on the activities of the Geneva Science and Diplomacy Anticipator (GESDA), which aims to link scientific and technological discoveries with diplomacy.

During their meeting with ICRC vice president Gilles Carbonnier, the Council presidents reiterated their support and the identification of the Swiss population with the institution. As the ICRC's third-largest contributor and a depositary state of the Geneva Conventions, Switzerland remains a unique partner for the ICRC, according to Mr Carbonnier. Discussions also addressed the ICRC's involvement in Ukraine and its difficulty, in a very polarised world, to preserve its neutrality in order to respond to humanitarian needs. Mr Carbonnier insisted on the importance of differentiating between the neutrality of an institution and that of a State and on the risk of linking the two. In Ukraine, ICRC teams provide assistance to municipalities in connection with electrical infrastructure and access to water. They also visit prisoners on both sides. The humanitarian aid provided by the ICRC in other conflicts (Ethiopia, Somalia, Libya, etc.) was also discussed, as well as its concerns regarding the Horn of Africa, which is currently experiencing near famine and great insecurity.

In the Council presidents’ meeting with the new UN High Commissioner for Human Rights (OHCHR), Volker Türk of Austria, topics discussed included human rights concerns in the context of current conflicts around the world. The High Commissioner emphasised the universality of human rights and the problems of disinformation that arise from the digitalisation of our societies. One of the priorities of the OHCHR is not to become an instrument of the geopolitics of States, even if the propaganda of certain countries tries to make people believe the contrary.

During their visit to Geneva, the Council presidents were able to see for themselves the progress of the Strategic Heritage Plan, which aims to renovate the Palais des Nations, modernise it and expand its capacities. As host state, Switzerland has granted loans for the construction and renovation of the real estate of the international organisations based in Geneva, thus ensuring their sustainability.

To conclude their visit to International Geneva, the Council presidents were received by the president of the Geneva cantonal government, Mauro Poggia, and Cantonal Councillor Nathalie Fontanet for a working lunch. In her speech, the National Council president welcomed the close cooperation between the Canton of Geneva and the Confederation, which makes it possible to offer favourable framework conditions for the development and sustainability of international organisations. She also expressed her conviction that it was precisely in Geneva that the humanitarian and democratic values that characterise Switzerland are best represented.

Meeting various International Geneva stakeholders enabled Ms Kälin and Mr Hefti, the highest representatives of the Swiss Parliament, to better understand how the presence of these organisations impacts Switzerland, particularly in terms of the country’s visibility on the world stage and Geneva’s essential role as a platform for debating important global issues.
