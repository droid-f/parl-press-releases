Negotiations on an EFTA-Mercosur free trade agreement set to begin

Representatives of the Swiss EFTA/EU delegation paid an official working visit to Argentina from 12 to 15 February. The focus of the talks on legislative and executive matters were bilateral economic relations and the start of negotiations on a free trade agreement between the member states of the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) and those of the Southern Common Market (Mercosur).

Content

​Within Latin America, Argentina is the third largest economy, and the most important investment location for Swiss businesses after Brazil and Mexico; it has also held the rotating presidency of Mercosur since 1 January. In the context of Argentine economic liberalisation, the members of the EFTA/EU Delegation noted a broad consensus in favour of a swift start and speedy conclusion to negotiations on a free trade agreement in their talks with members of the Argentine Congress and government ministers. A free trade agreement would boost Swiss competitiveness in the region, opening a market serving a population of around 250 million to Swiss businesses.

The starting gun for formal free trade negotiations with the Mercosur states Brazil, Argentina, Uruguay and Paraguay was fired on 19 January at the Annual Meeting of the World Economic Forum in Davos. National Council member Thomas Aeschi (SVP/ZG), president of the EFTA/EU Delegation, informed his Argentinian counterparts that a negotiating mandate has since been unanimously approved by the relevant committees in the Swiss parliament and praised the efforts of the Mercosur states to strengthen foreign trade relations.

During the three-day working visit, members of the EFTA/EU Delegation met, among others, the vice president of the Argentine Senate, Federico Pinedo, the vice president of the Chamber of Deputies, Patricia Giménez, the chairs of the Mercosur and budget committees of the Chamber of Deputies and the chair of the Senate foreign policy committee. Discussions at these meetings and with members of the Swiss-Argentinian Chamber of Commerce focused on both the political developments since Mauricio Macri‘s government took office and on opportunities to further intensify economic cooperation. In an exchange with the head of the Argentinian tax and customs authority, the AFIP, one issue raised was the joint declaration between Switzerland and Argentina on the introduction of a mutual automatic exchange of information on tax matters (AEOI).

National Council member Thomas Aeschi (SVP/ZG), President of the EFTA/EU Delegation, led the Swiss delegation, which also included Council of States members Karin Keller-Sutter (FDP/SG) and Didier Berberat (SP/NE) and National Council member Kathy Riklin (CVP/ZH).
