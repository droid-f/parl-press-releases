Press release Ottawa and Mexico City

Development and modernisation of the EFTA Free Trade Agreement with Canada and Mexico

The Parliamentary Committee of the European Free Trade Association (EFTA) has examined the possibility of developing and modernising the existing free trade agreement that Switzerland and its EFTA partners have with Canada and Mexico. A delegation from EFTA Parliamentary Committee, comprising representatives from the national parliaments of the EFTA states, held official talks on this matter in Ottawa (8–9 May 2017) and Mexico City (10 –11 May 2017) with members of parliament and with representatives of governments, national associations and international organisations.

Content

​On their two-day working visit to Ottawa, the members of the EFTA Parliamentary Committee were welcomed by Bruce Stanton, Deputy Speaker of the House of Commons, the Canadian lower house. They also held exploratory trade talks at parliamentary level with Pamela Goldsmith-Jones, the Parliamentary Secretary to the Minister for International Trade, and with the chairman and members of the House of Commons Standing Committees for International Trade, for Industry, Science and Technology, and for Transport, Infrastructure and Communities and with the chairman and members of the Senate Standing Committee for Foreign Affairs and International Trade.

In the course of the official meetings, the Swiss EFTA/EU Delegation, which represents Swiss interests within the EFTA Parliamentary Committee, tested the level of political will in Canada side for developing and modernising the existing free trade agreement. The free trade agreement that Switzerland and its EFTA partners concluded with Canada, which came into force on 1 July 2009, primarily covers trade in goods, and in particular the abolition of customs duties on industrial products.

The delegation focused on areas that are not or only inadequately covered by the existing free trade agreement, such as the trade in services, investments, public procurement, regulatory cooperation and trading in agricultural products.

The development and modernisation of the existing agreement aims primarily to prevent discrimination against Swiss exporters. Such discrimination could arise from preferential market access provisions in favour of competitors such as the EU. The EU and Canada signed a comprehensive free trade agreement (CETA) on 30 October 2016 that not only covers market access for the trade in goods, thus eliminating practically all customs duties on industrial and agricultural products, but also covers the trade in services, investments, public procurement, regulatory cooperation and intellectual property rights.

In the second half of the week, the members of the EFTA Parliamentary Committee focused on the ongoing revision of EFTA’s free trade agreement with Mexico, which came into force on 1 July 2001. Alongside provisions liberalising the trade in industrial products and services, this agreement also contains provisions on protecting and encouraging direct investments, on protecting intellectual property, on fair access to public procurement and on competition. The negotiations on the modernisation of the existing free trade agreement relate for example to the trade in goods and services, rules on origin, cooperation on customs matters, trade facilitation, public procurement and sustainable development.

At official meetings with economic affairs minister Ildefonso Guajardo Villarreal and former deputy foreign trade minister Francisco de Rosenzweig, the status of the current negotiations on the development and modernisation of the existing free trade agreement was discussed. In talks with government representatives, the members of the EFTA Parliamentary Committee expressed their wish to aim for a successful conclusion to negotiations by the end of the year. Trade policy issues were also the subject of talks with members of the parliamentary committees and members of the Mexican Entrepreneurial Council for Trade, Investment and Technology (COMCE). In addition, the Swiss EFTA/EU Delegation spoke with representatives of international organisations, in particular the UNHCHR, OECD and the World Bank.

Thomas Aeschi (SVP/ZG), member of the National Council and President of the EFTA/EU-Delegation, Karin Keller-Sutter (FDP/SG), member of the Council of States and Vice President of the EFTA/EU-Delegation, Eric Nussbaumer (SP/BL) member of the National Council, and Konrad Graber (CVP/LU), member of the Council of States were the members of the Swiss EFTA/EU Delegation on this working visit by the EFTA Parliamentary Committee, which is currently chaired by Svein Roald Hansen, Vice President of the Norwegian Parliament and chairman of the Norwegian EFTA/EEA Delegation.
