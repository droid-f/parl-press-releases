Review of the 36th EU-Switzerland Inter-parliamentary Meeting

The delegations responsible for EU-Switzerland relations from the Swiss Federal Assembly and the European Parliament held their 36th inter-parliamentary annual meeting in Unterägeri (ZG) on 21 and 22 September. Representatives from the two parliaments discussed bilateral relations between Switzerland and the EU with Claude Maerten, the head of the Western Europe Division of the European External Action Service (EEAS), and Urs Bucher, the head of the Swiss Mission to the EU in Brussels.

Content

​Under the co-chairmanship of National Council member Thomas Aeschi (SVP/ZG) and MEP Jørn Dohrmann (ECR, Denmark), the members of the two delegations discussed bilateral relations and the state of ongoing talks concerning institutional issues in connection with the bilateral agreements (further development, interpretation and monitoring of application of the agreements and dispute resolution) with Mr Claude Maerten, head of the Western Europe Division of the European External Action Service, and Ambassador Urs Bucher, head of the Swiss Mission to the EU in Brussels.

The chairs of the two delegations, National Council member Thomas Aeschi and MEP Jørn Dorhmann, agreed that in the context of the ongoing negotiations on an institutional framework agreement, the autonomy and integrity of the legal systems of both parties must be respected. They also remain convinced that the bilateral path is the best instrument for structured and scalable relations between Switzerland and the EU based on partnership.

The delegates of the 36th Inter-parliamentary annual meeting also addressed the issue of migration. Discussions focused on the situation in the central Mediterranean and in Libya, including migration routes to Libya, and return policy. With regard to the European migration agenda, delegates discussed immediate measures for saving human lives at sea, fighting against criminal trafficking networks and supporting countries on the EU’s external border in dealing with the high number of new arrivals.

The members of the EFTA/EU and SINEEA delegation also addressed the timetable, procedures and material aspects of the UK’s departure from the EU and discussed trends, opportunities and challenges relating to free trade.

The members of the delegations from the Federal Assembly and the European Parliament responsible for Swiss-EU relations have been meeting since 1981 to discuss topical events in Europe and in Switzerland. These meetings which take place alternately in Switzerland and the EU provide an opportunity to exchange views and information and thus foster mutual understanding between the two partners.
