Official visit to Burkina Faso by the president of the National Council

The president of the National Council, Andreas Aebi (SVP/BE), will visit Burkina Faso from 18 to 21 April with a Swiss parliamentary delegation. In Ouagadougou, he will take part in the ceremony to launch the Swiss cooperation programme in Burkina Faso for 2021‒25. Meetings with the president of the National Assembly and other Burkinabe authorities, as well as visits to Swiss cooperation projects, are on the agenda.

Content

Mr Aebi and the parliamentary delegation will be received by the president of the National Assembly, Alassane Bala Sakandé, who previously visited Bern in 2018 on the margins of the 138th Assembly of the Inter-Parliamentary Union in Geneva. They will also meet with members of parliamentary committees and other authorities in Burkina Faso.

The discussions will focus on the political situation following the parliamentary elections in November 2020, security in the region and the partnership between the two countries, particularly in development cooperation.

A large part of Mr Aebi's visit will be spent visiting projects, for example in the fields of local economic development, vocational education and training, and governance and democracy. Burkina Faso has been a priority country for Swiss international cooperation since 1974. Through its programmes in development cooperation, humanitarian aid and human security, Switzerland is working to find alternative responses to violent extremism in the Sahel region. Burkina Faso currently has over one million internally displaced people as a result of violence. Peacebuilding in the Sahel region is also one of the priorities set out in the Federal Council's 2021‒24 strategy for sub-Saharan Africa.

Switzerland recognised Burkina Faso's independence on 5 August 1960 and established diplomatic relations with the country in 1961. Since then, the two countries have concluded agreements on trade, investment protection, technical cooperation and budgetary aid. Switzerland's development cooperation programmes are primarily focused on the northern, central-western and eastern regions of the country. Since 1994, the State Secretariat for Economic Affairs (SECO) has provided budgetary aid and related macroeconomic support. The country also receives various forms of humanitarian and food aid either directly from Swiss Humanitarian Aid or through UN agencies or NGOs, depending on the needs identified.

The president of the National Council will be accompanied by National Council members Christine Badertscher (Greens/BE) and Eric Nussbaumer (SP/BL), both members of the National Council Foreign Affairs Committee.
